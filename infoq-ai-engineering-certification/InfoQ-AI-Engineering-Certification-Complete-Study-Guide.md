## Program Overview

### What is AI-Native Engineering?

AI-native engineering represents a fundamental shift in how software teams build, deploy, and maintain systems. Unlike traditional software engineering where AI is an add-on feature, AI-native engineering treats AI capabilities as **first-class citizens** in the architecture.

**Key Characteristics:**
- AI components are integral to system design from day one
- Teams operate with continuous experimentation and rapid iteration
- Data infrastructure and model management are core engineering concerns
- Risk management includes AI-specific failure modes
- Product thinking extends to model behavior and user-AI interaction

### Certification Objectives

By the end of this program, you will be able to:

✅ Define and implement AI-native engineering practices in your organization  
✅ Design and build production-grade RAG systems and context pipelines  
✅ Architect AI agent systems with proper safety and control mechanisms  
✅ Design AI platforms with cost-effective inference and observability  
✅ Implement evaluation frameworks for trust, reliability, and operational excellence  
✅ Lead capstone projects from prototype to production

## Learning Path

### Week 1: Foundation - AI-Native Engineering
**Focus:** Mindset, strategy, and organizational readiness

**Topics:**
- What "AI-native" really means (beyond the hype)
- Product thinking for AI systems
- SDLC integration patterns
- Risk management and resilience
- Maturity assessment
- Organizational friction points

**Outcome:** You'll be able to assess your team's AI maturity and create a roadmap for AI adoption.

### Week 2: Core Technical - RAG & Context Pipelines
**Focus:** The workhorse pattern of enterprise AI

**Topics:**
- RAG architecture deep dive
- Vector RAG vs Graph RAG
- Context engineering principles
- Memory systems (ephemeral vs durable)
- Knowledge graphs
- Production-grade retrieval design

**Outcome:** You'll design and build production-ready RAG systems that stay grounded and accurate.

### Week 3: Advanced Patterns - AI Agents
**Focus:** Agentic systems that don't break in production

**Topics:**
- Agent spectrum (tools → multi-agent systems)
- Orchestration patterns
- Control mechanisms and safety
- Memory and state management
- Autonomy vs certainty tradeoffs
- Failure mode prevention

**Outcome:** You'll architect robust agent systems with proper guardrails and escape hatches.

### Week 4: Infrastructure - AI Platforms
**Focus:** Production-ready infrastructure patterns

**Topics:**
- Inference gateway design
- GPU cost optimization
- Centralized vs federated architecture
- Observability and monitoring
- Platform strategy
- Workload routing

**Outcome:** You'll design scalable, cost-effective AI platforms for your organization.

### Week 5: Excellence - Evals, Trust & Capstone
**Focus:** Operational disciplines for production AI

**Topics:**
- Evaluation frameworks
- Trust and reliability engineering
- Rollout strategies
- Capstone project presentation
- Full-stack integration
- Production readiness

**Outcome:** You'll present a production-ready AI system design with comprehensive evaluation and safety measures.

## Week-by-Week Breakdown

### Week 1: Becoming an AI-Native Engineering Team

**Learning Objectives:**
- Define AI-native engineering and distinguish it from traditional software engineering
- Identify AI ambiguity in product requirements (Hilary Mason's framework)
- Understand pattern continuation vs. reasoning in language models (Naomi Saphra's warning)
- Map current SDLC and propose AI integration strategy
- Assess team maturity on AI adoption scale
- Design risk management approaches for AI systems

**Key Concepts:**
1. AI-Native Engineering Principles
2. Product Thinking for AI
3. SDLC Integration
4. Risk Management & Resilience
5. Maturity Assessment
6. Organizational Change Management

**Homework:** Map your SDLC with AI integration strategy, identify friction points, set boundaries, and define success metrics.

**Time Commitment:** 8-10 hours

---

**Learning Objectives:**

**Key Concepts:**
1. RAG Architecture Patterns
2. Retrieval Design Principles

## Assessment & Certification

### Weekly Assessments

Each week includes:
- **Practice Questions:** 15-20 multiple-choice and scenario-based questions
- **Hands-On Exercises:** 5-10 practical implementation tasks
- **Self-Assessment Checklist:** Verify your understanding
- **Discussion Tasks:** Group debates and design critiques

### Capstone Project (Week 5)

**Format:** 20-minute group presentation + 30-minute peer discussion

**Requirements:**
1. Full stack design documentation
2. Context retrieval pipeline design
3. Platform policies and governance
4. Failure containment strategies
5. Security policies
6. Evaluation loop design
7. One unresolved architectural question

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Technical depth and accuracy (30%)
- Practical feasibility (25%)
- Innovation and creativity (20%)
- Presentation quality (15%)
- Peer discussion participation (10%)

### Certification Requirements

To earn certification:
- ✅ Complete all weekly homework assignments
- ✅ Pass weekly quizzes (70% minimum)
- ✅ Submit and present capstone project
- ✅ Participate in peer discussions
- ✅ Demonstrate understanding in final Q&A
