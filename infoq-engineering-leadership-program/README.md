## Program Overview

The **InfoQ Certified Engineering Leadership Program** is a comprehensive, five-week online certification cohort designed for senior engineers and software architects with a minimum of five years of experience. This program bridges the gap between technical excellence and leadership effectiveness, equipping participants with the skills to lead teams, develop strategy, execute effectively, and build sustainable, resilient systems.

### Program Philosophy

> **"Technical excellence alone doesn't make a great engineering leader. Understanding organizational dynamics, strategy, execution, measurement, and operations is what separates good engineers from great technical leaders."**

This program takes a holistic approach to engineering leadership, combining:

- **Behavioral economics** and **industrial-organizational psychology** to understand organizations
- **Systems thinking** and **product management** for strategic thinking
- **Risk-driven development** and **delegation** for effective execution
- **Measurement frameworks** and **blameless accountability** for continuous improvement
- **Resilience engineering** and **technical sustainability** for long-term success

### What Makes This Program Unique

1. **Comprehensive Deep Dives:** Each week includes 3,000-5,000 words of detailed content with real-world examples, case studies, and practical frameworks
2. **Interactive Learning:** 50+ questions per week, practice exercises with solutions, and hands-on assignments
3. **Visual Learning:** Mermaid diagrams, flowcharts, and visual aids to clarify complex concepts
4. **Real-World Application:** Case studies from Google, Amazon, Netflix, and Spotify; practical exercises you can apply immediately
5. **Capstone Project:** Apply all five weeks of learning to a real problem in your organization

## Program Structure

### Format

- **Duration:** 5 weeks
- **Time Commitment:** 4 hours per week (20 hours total)
- **Delivery:** Online, self-paced with cohort discussions
- **Structure:** Each week pairs a QCon talk with a 4-hour live working session

### Weekly Pattern

Each week follows a consistent structure:

1. **Pre-Session Preparation** (1-2 hours)
   - Read the weekly tutorial document
   - Review QCon talk (recommended)
   - Complete pre-session exercises

2. **Live Working Session** (2 hours)
   - Cohort discussion
   - Peer learning
   - Facilitator guidance
   - Application to your context

3. **Post-Session Application** (1-2 hours)
   - Complete weekly homework
   - Apply concepts to your work
   - Prepare for next week

### Cohort Learning

You'll work alongside a small cohort of senior engineers and software architects from different companies, each with a minimum of five years of experience. This peer learning environment provides:

- **Diverse perspectives:** Learn from different industries and contexts
- **Shared challenges:** Common problems and solutions
- **Accountability:** Stay on track with peers
- **Networking:** Build relationships with other engineering leaders

## Weekly Breakdown

### Week 1: Organizational Foundations

**Focus:** Being a good technical leader means understanding how organizations work, how to influence them, and how to change people's behavior.

**Key Topics:**
- Westrum's Organizational Culture Model (Pathological, Bureaucratic, Generative)
- Behavioral economics in organizations (loss aversion, social proof, anchoring)
- Industrial-organizational psychology (Maslow, Herzberg, Self-Determination Theory)
- Psychological safety and team dynamics
- Mapping formal vs. informal organizational structure
- Influence without authority

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Assess your organization's culture using Westrum's model
- Identify informal power structures and decision-making pathways
- Apply behavioral economics principles to influence change
- Map your organization's structure and decision flows
- Navigate different organizational cultures effectively

**Homework:** Map your part of the organization. Where do decisions actually get made, who influences them, and where does your read differ from the org chart? Bring one place where the informal structure and the formal one disagree.

**📄 [Week 1 Detailed Tutorial](week-1-organizational-foundations.md)**

---

### Week 2: Technical Strategy

**Focus:** How to identify a business problem that matters, analyze it, and build a (socio)technical strategy for it.

**Key Topics:**
- Systems thinking for technical leaders
- Identifying business problems that matter
- Building socio-technical strategies
- Product management for engineers (RICE, MoSCoW, JTBD)
- Software architecture strategy (ADRs, trade-off analysis)
- Strategic patterns (Strangler Fig, Anti-Corruption Layer)

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Apply systems thinking to analyze complex technical problems
- Identify business problems worth solving
- Frame technical challenges in business terms
- Build comprehensive (socio)technical strategies
- Make explicit trade-offs and document rejected options
- Define success metrics and validation criteria

**Homework:** Draft a one-page (socio)technical strategy for a current problem in your organization. Name the tradeoff you are choosing and the option you are rejecting, and say what would tell you the strategy is wrong.

**📄 [Week 2 Detailed Tutorial](week-2-technical-strategy.md)**

---

### Week 3: Technical Execution

**Focus:** Ideas are free. Execution is everything. How to execute a technical strategy through risk-driven development, delegation, and alignment mechanisms.

**Key Topics:**
- Risk-driven development (prioritize by risk, not just value)
- Delegation and empowerment (5 levels of delegation)
- Execution frameworks (Scrum, Kanban, Lean, OKRs, DORA metrics)
- Strategy-execution alignment (Hoshin Kanri, roadmaps)
- The last responsible moment
- Build-measure-learn loops

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Apply risk-driven development to prioritize work effectively
- Identify and mitigate risks early
- Delegate work effectively while maintaining accountability
- Choose appropriate execution frameworks
- Implement mechanisms to keep work aligned with strategy
- Make decisions at the right time and level

**Homework:** Write a plan for solving a problem in your organization. The plan should demonstrate a risk-driven approach: work is ordered by risk, risks have a set of mitigation options, and decisions are intentionally delayed to the latest responsible moment.

**📄 [Week 3 Detailed Tutorial](week-3-technical-execution.md)**

---

### Week 4: Measurement and Accountability

**Focus:** How to tell whether your technical strategy is working, and how to maintain accountability while keeping blamelessness and psychological safety intact.

**Key Topics:**
- Measuring technical strategy success (outcomes, behaviors, knowledge, participation)
- Accountability vs. blame
- Blamelessness and psychological safety
- Metrics frameworks (North Star, HEART, OKRs, DORA, Balanced Scorecard)
- Avoiding measurement pitfalls (Goodhart's Law, outcomes bias, vanity metrics)
- Leading vs. lagging indicators

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Design measurement frameworks for technical strategies
- Distinguish between leading and lagging indicators
- Apply blameless accountability in practice
- Balance accountability with psychological safety
- Avoid common measurement pitfalls
- Combine quantitative and qualitative measurement

**Homework:** Brainstorm a list of potential ways of measuring the success of a business problem you are solving. Classify them as whether they measure participation, knowledge, behavior, or outcomes. For each measure, describe a way you would also do qualitative sampling to avoid the consequences of Goodhart's Law.

**📄 [Week 4 Detailed Tutorial](week-4-measurement-and-accountability.md)**

---

### Week 5: Safety, Sustainability, and Resilience

**Focus:** The software development lifecycle does not end when the code is deployed. Maintenance and operational costs often dwarf the original development cost.

**Key Topics:**
- Full software system lifecycle (design → operate → maintain → decommission)
- Capacity planning and performance optimization
- Support and operational costs (TCO, 10x rule)
- Incident response and management (SEV levels, runbooks, post-mortems)
- System resilience and reliability (circuit breakers, bulkheads, graceful degradation)
- Technical sustainability and debt management (70-20-10 rule)
- Capstone project presentations

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Apply full lifecycle thinking to software systems
- Plan for capacity and scale effectively
- Estimate and manage operational costs
- Design incident response processes
- Build resilient systems that withstand failures
- Manage technical sustainability and debt
- Present a comprehensive capstone project

**Capstone Project:** The capstone is introduced in Week 1 and built throughout the program. The deliverable is a 20-minute group presentation followed by a peer discussion. End on a privacy or security question your group is still working out.

**📄 [Week 5 Detailed Tutorial](week-5-safety-sustainability-resilience.md)**

## Capstone Project

### Overview

The capstone project is introduced in Week 1 and built throughout the program. It's your opportunity to apply all five weeks of learning to a real problem in your organization.

### Requirements

**Deliverable:** 20-minute group presentation (2-3 people per group)

**Presentation Structure:**
1. **Problem Definition** (3 min)
   - Business problem you're addressing
   - Why it matters
   - Impact and urgency

2. **Organizational Analysis** (3 min)
   - Westrum culture assessment
   - Informal vs. formal structure
   - Key stakeholders and influencers

3. **Technical Strategy** (4 min)
   - Systems thinking analysis
   - Proposed solution
   - Trade-offs and rejected options
   - Success metrics

4. **Execution Plan** (4 min)
   - Risk-driven approach
   - Top 5 risks and mitigations
   - Delegation plan
   - Timeline

5. **Measurement Framework** (3 min)
   - Success metrics (outcomes, behaviors, knowledge, participation)
   - Baselines and targets
   - How you'll know you're wrong

6. **Operations and Sustainability** (2 min)
   - Capacity planning
   - Operational costs
   - Incident response
   - Technical debt management

7. **Privacy/Security Question** (1 min)
   - Open question your group is still working through
   - Prompt peer discussion

### Evaluation Criteria

**Content (40%):**
- Problem clearly defined and quantified
- Comprehensive organizational analysis
- Sound technical strategy with explicit trade-offs
- Risk-driven execution plan
- Measurement framework with leading/lagging indicators
- Operations and sustainability considerations

**Presentation (30%):**
- Clear and engaging delivery
- Effective use of time
- Visual aids (slides, diagrams)
- Answers to questions
- Peer discussion facilitation

**Integration (30%):**
- Applies all 5 weeks of learning
- Shows progression and depth
- Real-world applicability
- Thoughtful privacy/security question
- Actionable recommendations

### Capstone Tips

**Do:**
- ✅ Start early (Week 1)
- ✅ Apply frameworks from each week
- ✅ Use real data and examples
- ✅ Practice your presentation
- ✅ Engage the audience
- ✅ Be honest about challenges

**Don't:**
- ❌ Wait until Week 5
- ❌ Focus only on technology
- ❌ Ignore social aspects
- ❌ Over-promise
- ❌ Avoid difficult questions
- ❌ Make it theoretical

## Frequently Asked Questions

### General Questions

**Q: Is this program right for me if I'm an individual contributor (IC)?**
A: Yes! This program is designed for senior ICs who are considering or transitioning to leadership roles. It helps you develop leadership skills while remaining technical.

**Q: What if I'm already an engineering manager?**
A: This program will strengthen your technical leadership skills, help you develop better strategies, and provide frameworks for effective execution and measurement.

**Q: How is this different from other leadership programs?**
A: This program is specifically designed for technical leaders. It combines organizational psychology, systems thinking, technical strategy, execution, measurement, and operations - all from a technical perspective.

**Q: Do I need to have a specific tech stack or background?**
A: No. The concepts and frameworks are technology-agnostic and apply to any software engineering context.

### Logistics Questions

**Q: What if I miss a live session?**
A: Sessions are recorded and available within 24 hours. However, active participation is required for certification (attend at least 4 of 5 sessions).

**Q: Can I go at my own pace?**
A: The program is cohort-based with scheduled live sessions. However, you can complete readings and homework on your own schedule within the week.

**Q: Is there a group project?**
A: Yes, the capstone project is completed in groups of 2-3 people. This simulates real-world collaboration and peer learning.

**Q: What if English is not my first language?**
A: All materials are in English. The program is designed to be accessible to non-native speakers with clear, straightforward language.

### Career Questions

**Q: Will this help me get promoted?**
A: This program provides the skills and frameworks that organizations look for in senior and lead roles. Many participants report promotions within 6-12 months of completion.

**Q: Is this recognized by employers?**
A: InfoQ is a leading voice in the software development industry. This certification demonstrates commitment to professional development and leadership excellence.

**Q: What if I don't want to become a manager?**
A: This program is valuable for staff/principal engineers, tech leads, and architects who want to lead without managing. Many leadership skills are applicable to IC roles.
